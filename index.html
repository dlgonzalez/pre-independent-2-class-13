Grammar Lab · Step 1

Defining Relative Clauses: discover the pattern

Connect two ideas

Idea A

Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin.

Idea B

Penicillin is a medicine.

Combine them to identify exactly which medicine:
Penicillin is the medicine that Alexander Fleming discovered.

The clause that Alexander Fleming discovered tells us exactly which medicine we mean. It identifies the noun; it is not just extra information.

Notice the pattern

WHO · people

The inventor who designed the telephone was Alexander Graham Bell.

WHICH · things

A stethoscope is an instrument which doctors use to listen to the heart.

WHERE · places

The laboratory where the experiment took place was in New Jersey.

Structure: noun + relative word + clause. The relative clause comes immediately after the noun it describes.
Grammar Lab · Step 2

Your Defining Relative Clauses toolkit

1. Choose the relative word

WHO

People

Louis Braille was the man who developed the reading system.

WHICH

Things / animals

A camera is a device which records images.

THAT

People or things in defining clauses

The invention that changed medicine was revolutionary.

WHERE

Places

This is the laboratory where scientists tested the material.

WHOSE

Possession

The inventor whose idea inspired the team became famous.

WHEN

Time expressions

2007 was the year when the iPhone appeared.

2. Subject or object? Essential difference

SUBJECT relative pronoun

The scientist who invented the device…

The pronoun performs the action. Do not omit it.

OBJECT relative pronoun

The device (that) the scientist invented…

The scientist performs the action; the device receives it. You can omit who / which / that when it is the object.

✔ The engineer who designed the bridge won a prize. (subject)
✔ The bridge (which) the engineer designed won a prize. (object)
✘ The engineer designed the bridge won a prize.

3. Word order

NOUN + RELATIVE WORD + SUBJECT + VERB + EXTRA INFORMATION

The tool that doctors use to examine patients is called a stethoscope.

4. No commas

Defining relative clauses do not have commas because the information is necessary to identify the noun.

The students who watched the video will share their summaries.
The clause identifies which students. Do not put commas around it.

5. Common mistakes

  • ✘ The inventor which created… → ✔ The inventor who created…
  • ✘ The device who helps… → ✔ The device that / which helps…
  • ✘ The place which I was born… → ✔ The place where I was born…
  • ✘ The scientist who's invention… → ✔ The scientist whose invention…
  • ✘ The machine that it changed… → ✔ The machine that changed…

Presentation planner · Follow this model

Opening: “My video is about ______, which is used for ______.”
Inventor: “It was invented by ______, who ______.”
Background: “Before this invention appeared, people had ______.”
Story: “By the time ______ happened, the inventor had already ______.”
Impact: “It became important because ______.”
Opinion: “I think this invention is ______ because ______.”

Useful language

  • The invention which / that…
  • The person who…
  • The place where…
  • The inventor whose…
  • Before…, people had…
  • By the time…, the inventor had…
  • In my opinion… / What surprised me was… / I believe…

Small-group sharing

  1. Take turns presenting your invention (about 1–2 minutes each).
  2. Listeners: identify one interesting fact and one defining relative clause.
  3. Ask a follow-up question about the invention or its impact.
  4. Compare the inventions: Which one has had the greatest impact? Explain why.
Final language challenge: Try to use at least two defining relative clauses and two Past Perfect sentences in your summary and discussion. Focus on clear meaning first, then accuracy.
